Why is your Mac sweating?

MacSweat lives in your menu bar and names what’s heating up or slowing down your Mac, down to the dev server you forgot to stop.

curl -fsSL https://macsweat.parthh.dev/install | sh

Free and open source. Mac only, macOS 13 or later.

    What the panel shows

    This is the real panel, captured from the app with sample data.

    • The culprit, in one sentenceIt names what’s using the most CPU and memory. The dot beside it turns green, orange or red.
    • What you left runningDev servers and scripts, with their project and how long they’ve been up. Hover a row to stop one. MacSweat asks first, and it never closes anything by itself.
    • Apps without the helper soupChrome’s 25 helpers count as Chrome. macOS services get a plain name and a line about what they’re doing.
    • Nothing leaves your MacNo account, no analytics. The only network request is the optional Check for Updates menu item.

    Install MacSweat

    Paste this into Terminal. It downloads the latest release from GitHub, checks it, and puts MacSweat in your Applications folder.

    curl -fsSL https://macsweat.parthh.dev/install | sh

    v1.0.0 · macOS 13 or later · Read the script ·

    Rather not use Terminal? Download MacSweat.zip, then move MacSweat to Applications. It isn’t notarized by Apple, so macOS blocks it the first time you open it: go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
